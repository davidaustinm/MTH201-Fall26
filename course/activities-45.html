    Instructions: The exercises here should be completed in groups of 2 or 3 students with one write-up submitted from each group. You may write directly on this handout. When asked for an explanation, please use complete sentences. Be sure to include everyone’s name on the write-up.
    🔗
    Up to this point, we’ve have looked at differential equations that describe a single function. It sometimes happens, however, that we find two quantities whose rates of change depend on one another. In this case, we have a system of differential equations.
    🔗
    In today’s lab, we’ll begin by looking at a system of differential equations that can be used to describe the interaction of two different species, one of whom preys on the other. For instance, we will consider a population of wolves \(W(t)\) that prey upon a population of deer \(D(t)\text{.}\) Let’s build two differential equations that describe how the populations change.
    🔗
    First of all, we will suppose that if there were no wolves, the deer would increase at a rate proportional to their population:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dD}{dt} = 0.08D \quad\mbox{or}\quad\frac{dD/dt}{D} = 0.08 \end{equation*}
    to be precise where time \(t\) is measured in days. Notice this means that the per capita growth rate is constant.
    🔗
    If there are now some wolves around, we would expect that the per capita growth rate would decrease. For instance, we might find that
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dD/dt}{D} = (0.08 - 0.001W)\quad\mbox{or}\quad \frac{dD}{dt} = (0.08 - 0.001W)D. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    In the same way, we will suppose that if there were no deer, the wolves would die off, since they have no food, at a rate proportional to their population:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dW}{dt} = -0.02W. \end{equation*}
    However, if there are some deer around, we expect the wolves’ per capita growth rate to increase. For instance,
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dW/dt}{W} = (-0.02 + 0.00002D)\quad\mbox{or}\quad \frac{dW}{dt} = (-0.02 + 0.00002D)W. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The pair of equations
    \begin{align*} \frac{dD}{dt} \amp = \amp (0.08 - 0.001W) D\\ \frac{dW}{dt} \amp = \amp (-0.02 + 0.00002D)W \end{align*}
    is called the Lotka-Volterra model for predator-prey interaction. Though a simple model, the basic effects predicted by the model have been observed in nature.
    🔗

    2.

    (a)

    We’ll now examine another system of differential equations but now we will imagine we have two species \(x\) and \(y\) that compete for food. If there is one species without the other, we will assume a logistic model for population growth: say,
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dx}{dt}=x(1-x) \end{equation*}
    where \(x\) is measured in millions. However, if there is some of the species \(y\) around, it will lower the growth rate of \(x\) to, say,
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dx}{dt}=x(1-x-y). \end{equation*}
    Species \(y\) behaves in the same way:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dy}{dt}=y(0.75-y-0.5x) \end{equation*}
    so we have the system of equations
    \begin{align*} \frac{dx}{dt}\amp=\ampx(1-x-y)\\ \frac{dy}{dt}\amp=\ampy(0.75-y-0.5x) \end{align*}
    🔗
    Set up a spreadsheet to study this system of equations with the intial values \(x(0) = 1.5\) and \(y(0)=0.25\) using a step size of \(h=0.1\text{.}\) Make a plot of \(x(t)\) and \(y(t)\text{.}\) What happens after a very long time?
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Study what happens with a few different values of the initial values. What do you notice happens here?
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    If the constants are adjusted just a little bit, there is a very different kind of behavior. Consider the system of equations
    \begin{align*} \frac{dx}{dt}\amp=\ampx(1-x-y)\\ \frac{dy}{dt}\amp=\ampy(0.5-0.25y-0.75x) \end{align*}
    🔗
    Study this system with \(x(0) = 1.5\) and \(y(0)=0.25\text{.}\) Again, make a plot of \(x(t)\) and \(y(t)\text{.}\) What happens after a very long time?
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Now consider the initial values \(x(0) = 0.5\) and \(y(0)=1\text{.}\) What happens after a very long time?
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    How do these two examples of systems of competing species differ from one another?
